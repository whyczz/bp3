# HN and Lobsters practitioner reports on coding-agent harnesses

Scope: comment threads on Hacker News and Lobsters, roughly 2025-01 through 2026-11-23, weighted to 2026. A comment is evidence of what that person says they do, not proof it works. "Linked" means the comment itself points at a repo, gist, or issue. "Hearsay" means an unsourced personal claim. Comment scores are not in the Firebase item API or the Algolia comment hits used here; reply counts are a weak engagement signal only. Story points are from Algolia or Firebase.

Sampling limits, stated once: the 2026-11-04 Ask HN was not read in full. The first 50 of 504 ranked top-level comments were fetched (HN kids order, not a random sample of all descendants). Other threads: the first 10–18 ranked top-level comments, plus a few replies already in Algolia pages. Lobsters HTML is behind Anubis; pages below were opened in a browser. Unread comment tails are gaps, not silence.

## What harness shapes do commenters say they run day to day?

### Takeaway
On the November 2026 Ask HN, the visible majority describe one Claude or Codex session (often desktop, sometimes CLI) plus a short plan, not a swarm. A smaller set runs worktrees, per-task fresh sessions, or an issue tracker as the queue. Ralph loops, Gas Town, and custom orchestrators show up as named experiments with public repos, and as things people say they tried and dropped.

### Inferences
- "One long session" and "fresh context per task" are both claimed. The people who stayed simple describe one Claude/Codex thread and a definition of done. The people who describe hours of unattended work describe a ticket graph or a loop whose exit condition is a test, not a longer chat.
- Worktrees are the concrete answer when someone asks how N agents avoid colliding. Several of those same threads also say one human cannot hold N features in their head (throwaw12, below).
- Pi shows up in 2026-11 as the harness people switch to when they want a small context and open models. OpenCode is mentioned mostly as the thing they left.
- Issue trackers (GitHub issues, Beads-like markdown, Backlog.md, personal kanban) are the queue. Gas Town is discussed as a spectacle, not as "what I run on Monday" in this sample, except vessenes's weekend (costs section).

- No count was made of how many of the 504 say "Claude only" versus "swarm." The split above is from the first 50, not a census.
- Lobsters search for `claude code` (no quotes) returned 840 badly tokenized hits and was not usable. Quote search `"coding agent"` was usable. ## What costs do they report: tokens, time per feature, review burden, giving up, multi-agent slower?

### Takeaway
Reported money ranges from a $20 Claude plan, to under $100/month for a three-model split that the author says is about 3x slower than one-shot, to $2k/month for one or two agents, to several $500 Codex plans plus a 20-machine fleet. Review burden is the cost people describe even when tokens are free: bigger diffs, overbuilt patches, and a human who no longer knows the code.

## Where do HN and Lobsters regulars push back on multi-agent, LLM graders, and heavy orchestration?

### Takeaway
The pushback is not "models cannot code." It is: swarms have no quality numbers, a second LLM as judge often fails to converge or adds a flaky check, and orchestration posts celebrate lines of code. The alternative they name is a deterministic check, a smaller diff, or one agent that tests its own work.

## Is there a consensus on the cheapest loop that still catches bugs?

### Takeaway
No single recipe. The overlap among skeptics of orchestration is: a deterministic check (types, lint, a test the model did not grade), a human on the design or on a small diff, and a second model only as a cheap first pass on the diff. A second model with its own prompt, used as the gate, is the thing they call a flaky test. Same-session tests that the coding agent also wrote are treated as suspect on Lobsters.

### Cited Findings
- Deterministic stack, named as such. hliyan, 2026-11-18: "Formal verification is just one, extreme form of deterministic verification. Linting, compilation, automated tests are all deterministic." — [HN 50541131](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50541131)

### Inferences
- The cheapest loop this sample actually describes, when people are trying not to spend a second full agent, is: human names the behavior, a deterministic test or linter fails closed, and the human looks at the spec or a small diff. A second model is added on the diff by people who already pay for two subscriptions (gregwebs) or who have a review team (sotander), and those people do not call it cheap.
- "Tests don't review" matches kornel, cr8, and thefunnyman more than it matches alkonaut or VadimPR. The consensus is only that a test the author-agent wrote in the same session is not a review.
- Fresh context shows up as the cheap trick: new session to attack the diff (kornel), focused session per ticket (wild_egg), Ralph clearing history until CI is green (jes5199). That is not the same as a multi-agent org chart.

### Gaps
- Nobody in the fetched comments published a head-to-head of "tests only" vs "tests plus a second model on the diff" vs "human on every line" with bug counts. sotander's "better together" is a 23-year-experience claim plus a team, not a table.
- Dan Luu's verification article is the obvious primary source behind Lobsters adrda9. The two comments say the post is interesting and under-specified. The post was not opened, so its numbers are not in this note.
- FartyMcFarter's remarks on hermetic tests (50530251, 50546461) are about test design, not about a harness he runs. Left out of the "what I run" list on purpose.
